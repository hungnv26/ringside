# Ringside graphics plan

Drafted 2026-10-30 at the user's request: "every element in the game is too basic; I want it to
look very detailed and comprehensible". The user chose **grounded realism** with fighters built
from **MakeHuman / MPFB2**. Phases 0 and 1 are built (2026-10-30); see "Progress" at the end.

## Where it stands

What the game is made of today, from the repo and from rendered stills of every stage and
fighter:

- **Fighters.** Every fighter is one of two Quaternius bodies (about 14,000 triangles, 2K
  textures), so all seven share two faces with different hair. Clothing is generated by
  `tools/build_outfits.gd` as a shell over the body: no folds, seams or weight, so a gi reads
  as plastic. Hands are low detail. Shading is already careful (wrapped diffuse for skin, eye
  catchlight, hair anisotropy, pore normal map) but is limited by the meshes underneath.
- **Stages.** Floors and walls use good scanned Poly Haven textures, but most props are
  primitives placed by the `tools/build_*_stage.gd` scripts: 154 primitive meshes on the Ring
  alone, cone trees and sphere clouds on the Express and Temple, box buildings, and spectators
  that are coloured pills. That is the main source of the "basic" look.
- **Lighting.** No ambient occlusion, no indirect light, no baked light, screen-space
  reflections off, one reflection probe on two stages. Objects float on the floor with no
  contact darkening, and every surface is lit the same way.
- **Effects.** Particle sparks, coloured rings and a limb ribbon. No textures, smoke, distortion
  or impact frames. Hair, belts and clothing are rigid.
- **HUD.** Godot's default font, flat bars, no portraits by the health bars.

The good news: the frame is already 1080p-plus, the stages use the right kind of textures, and
the shading code is built to take better meshes. The fix is better content, better light, and
motion on the parts that should move.

## Two decisions first

**1. Art direction.**

| Option | Look | Fits what exists | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|---|
| A. Grounded realism | Tekken 8: realistic people, scanned materials, cinematic light | Stages already use scanned textures; shading is already physically based | **Recommended** |
| B. Stylized detail | Street Fighter 6 / Arc System: painted textures, bold shapes, strong outlines | Keeps the Quaternius proportions; needs every stage repainted to match | Only if you prefer the style |

**2. Where the new fighters come from.** The repo is public, so every asset in it must be
redistributable (CC0 or CC BY), as today.

| Option | Quality | Licence in a public repo | Cost and risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. MakeHuman / MPFB2 in Blender | Realistic bodies, faces, skin, eyes, hair, clothes; one unique face per fighter | CC0 assets and exports: fine | Free. Needs Blender installed, a retarget of the current animations onto the new rig (Godot's humanoid retargeting), and the face blend shapes rebuilt. **Recommended** |
| 2. Upgrade the current Quaternius bodies | Better clothes, hair cards, textures, moving hair and belts; faces stay two | Already CC0 | Lowest risk, smallest lift |
| 3. Commercial characters (Character Creator 4, marketplace fighters) | Highest | Raw assets cannot be published: the repo would go private or keep them out of git | Paid licences |
| 4. AI-generated 3D (Meshy, Tripo, Rodin) | Fast unique looks | Depends on the plan's terms | Topology, rigs and faces are weak for close-up fighting; fine for background props |

## Phases

Each phase ends with before-and-after stills from fixed cameras (the existing
`tools/render_stage_thumbs.gd` and `tools/render_portraits.gd`) and a frame-rate check on this
Mac with `tools/bench_shading.gd`.

### Phase 0 · Baseline and budget (1 session)

- Capture the "before" set: every stage from the fight camera, every fighter close up.
- Measure frame time at 3200×1800 on the M1 Pro for each stage.
- Add a **Showcase** quality preset for coached matches and recordings. Recording uses Godot's
  movie writer at a fixed 60 fps, so a recorded match can use settings far too heavy for live
  play and still come out smooth.
- Price every effect and upscaler on this Mac before choosing presets.
- Install Blender if option 1 is chosen.

### Phase 1 · Light and rendering (1–2 sessions, lifts every stage at once)

- Ambient occlusion and screen-space indirect light, so fighters and props sit on the floor.
- Global illumination at Showcase (SDFGI). Baked lightmaps were dropped: Godot only bakes
  them from the editor, by hand, after giving every primitive a second UV set, which the stage
  builders would have to redo on every rebuild.
- Screen-space reflections where the floor is polished or wet: rooftop deck, dojo wood, ring.
- Soft shadows that sharpen at contact (light size on the key light), a larger shadow atlas.
- Upscaling that pays for the light: the 3D at 1080p, MetalFX spatial up to the window.
- A colour grade per stage (a lookup table), so each stage has its own mood.

### Phase 2 · Fighters (4–6 sessions, the biggest visible change)

- New bodies and heads from the chosen source: seven distinct faces with the expression blend
  shapes rebuilt (blink, squint, brow, jaw, smile, grimace), detailed hands, 4K skin textures.
- Modelled clothing with real folds, seams, stitching and belts, cloth materials from the
  existing Poly Haven fabric scans.
- Hair as layered cards with transparency instead of solid shells.
- **Secondary motion**: hair, belts, headbands, sashes and loose clothing swing with spring
  bones (Godot's SpringBoneSimulator3D). This is the single change that makes fighters feel
  alive.
- Real subsurface scattering on skin in the Showcase preset; the current stand-in stays for
  live play.
- Wear during a match: sweat sheen that builds over the rounds, scuff decals on hit areas.
- Re-render portraits and super cut-ins from the new models.

### Phase 3 · Stages (2 sessions for a pilot, then about 1 per stage)

- Pilot one stage end to end (the Ring or the Dojo), then apply the same recipe to the other
  seven.
- Replace primitive props with modelled CC0 assets: Poly Haven models and textures, ambientCG
  materials, Quaternius packs where a stylized piece fits. Cone trees, sphere clouds, box
  buildings and plank floors go.
- Decals for wear: scuffs, stains, cracks, posters, tape marks on the ring canvas.
- Foliage that moves in wind, real cloud layers or HDRI backplates instead of sphere clouds.
- **Crowds**: real low-poly people from the same character source, four or five cheering and
  idle loops baked into vertex animation and drawn with MultiMesh, with distant rows as
  impostor cards. No more pills.
- Background life per stage: passing traffic, flags, steam, birds, train scenery that streams.

### Phase 4 · Effects and motion (2 sessions)

- Hit sparks from textured flipbooks with shaded smoke, sized by damage and counter hits.
- Impact frames on heavy hits and supers: one or two frames of high contrast, a short
  heat-distortion shockwave, camera shake tuned per hit weight.
- Super moves: a slow camera push-in, background darkened, a brief slow-motion on the finisher.
- Dust and debris on knockdowns and wall splats, footprints on sand and gravel stages.
- A slow-motion replay of the K.O. from a cinematic angle before the round card.

### Phase 5 · HUD and presentation (1–2 sessions)

- A real typeface under the SIL Open Font licence for every label, set at proper sizes.
- Health bars with chip damage and a damage trail, fighter portraits beside the bars, styled
  round pips and combo counter.
- The corner talk, plan chips and round card restyled as panels that match the HUD.
- Round intro cameras that show each fighter and the stage before "Fight".
- Kept from the existing conventions: real data only, no neon, no strong glow, no moving
  lights on the HUD.

### Phase 6 · Polish loop (ongoing)

- Per-stage reviews against the before stills, performance checks, refreshed stage
  thumbnails, portraits and cut-ins, credits updated for every new asset.

## Constraints

- **Licences.** Every new asset is listed in `assets/CREDITS.md`; CC BY assets also on the
  in-game credits screen. Nothing that cannot be redistributed goes into the public repo.
- **Performance.** Live play must hold 60 fps on this Mac at the 1080p window; the Showcase
  preset and recordings may go further. Each phase checks frame time before it is committed.
- **Animations.** The Universal Animation Library moves stay; a new rig means retargeting, not
  re-animating. Gameplay and hit boxes are untouched by any phase.
- **Git LFS.** New textures and models go through LFS like the current ones; 4K textures add
  up, so stages share materials where they can.

## Recommended order

Phase 0, then Phase 1 (a big, cheap lift on every stage), then the Phase 3 pilot stage, then
Phase 2 (the longest), then Phases 4 and 5. Starting with light means the stage and fighter
work is judged under the final lighting instead of the current flat light.

## Progress

### Phases 0 and 1 (built 2026-10-30)

- `tools/look_capture.gd` shoots every stage from the fight camera (neutral and 6 s into a
  CPU-vs-CPU fight) and benchmarks each stage, in a coached match's window (1080p in screen
  points, 3200×1800 pixels here). `LOOK_*` variables switch single effects on to price them.
  Stills: `docs/look/before/`, `docs/look/after/` (High), `docs/look/showcase/`, and
  `docs/look/phase1_compare.jpg`.
- Prices on the M1 Pro at 3200×1800 native, from about 18 ms a frame: ambient occlusion
  +2.5 ms, screen-space indirect light +6 ms, reflections +5 ms, SDFGI +9 ms, VoxelGI +12 ms.
  FSR 2 cost more than it saved; MetalFX temporal broke with reflections (21 to 42 fps);
  MetalFX spatial at 60% doubled the frame rate and is nearly indistinguishable from native
  at the fight camera.
- **High** (default, live): the 3D at 1080p, MetalFX spatial up to the window, 4× MSAA,
  ambient occlusion with contact darkening, screen-space indirect light, reflections on the
  Dojo, Rooftop and Express floors, a slightly soft sun. 74 to 93 fps on every stage, up from
  53 to 73 fps before with none of it.
- **Showcase** (recordings): the window's full resolution, everything in High plus SDFGI and
  softer shadow filtering. 23 to 40 fps live; a recording renders every frame anyway. The
  setup page switches to it when *Record the match to video* is ticked.
- Per-stage light lives in `scripts/stages/stage_look.gd` (applied at run time to a copy of
  the stage's environment, so the generated stage scenes are untouched). The Ring keeps SDFGI
  off: its stands have no lamp and go black without the flat ambient light. A large sun
  (1.2°) washed the Temple's long shadows out, so suns stay at 0.3 to 0.35°.
- Coached matches take `--graphics=high|showcase`; the adapter passes `--graphics`, the setup
  page has *Picture quality*, tournaments carry it.
- Blender 5.2.3 is installed for Phase 2.
- What Phase 1 does not fix: at the fight camera the change is modest. The basic look is the
  content (primitive props, pill crowds, two shared faces), which is Phases 2 and 3.

### Phase 2 pilot: Jin (built 2026-10-30)

Jin is the first MakeHuman fighter; the other six still use the Quaternius bodies.

- **Setup on a new machine**: Blender 5.2 (`brew install --cask blender`), the MPFB extension
  (`Blender --online-mode --command extension install --enable mpfb`), and MakeHuman's CC0
  system asset pack (makehuman_system_assets_cc0.zip, 267 MB, from the page in
  `assets/CREDITS.md`) installed into MPFB's user data folder.
- **Build**: `Blender -b --python tools/mpfb/build_fighter.py -- tools/mpfb/fighters/jin.json`
  writes `assets/characters/mh/jin/` (glTF, about 10 MB, and its textures). A fighter is one
  small JSON file: macros (gender, age, muscle, weight, proportions, height, ethnic mix),
  skin and mirror-match skin, eyes, brows, lashes, hair, teeth, tongue.
- **Skeleton**: the MakeHuman limbs are posed into the game skeleton's T-pose and baked;
  the game skeleton is then rebuilt with each bone at the new body's joint and its own
  orientation (local rest rotations match within 0.06°), so all 94 existing animation clips
  play unchanged. Apply scale per object, never through a parent: double scaling put the
  finger pivots in the wrong place and stretched curled fists into planks.
- **Face**: blink, squint, brow_down, brow_up, jaw_open, smile and grimace come from
  MakeHuman's expression units, refitted onto brows, lashes, teeth and tongue; four gaze
  shapes turn each eyeball about its centre.
- **Gi**: jacket and trousers are cut from the body's own surface (so they inherit its
  weights), pushed out with looser sleeves and flared legs, given folds along the limbs and
  bunching at elbows and knees, hems turned inward for thickness; a collar band of constant
  width follows the neckline; a belt with knot and two ends. Skin hidden under the cloth is
  deleted. Materials `MI_Cloth_main/trim/accent` take the character's outfit colours and
  fabric maps in the game (`FighterModel._dress_model_cloth`), so mirror matches still swap
  colours.
- **Cost**: body about 107,000 triangles (one subdivision); one MakeHuman fighter costs 2 to
  3 fps at High (72 to 89 fps on every stage).
- **Known weak spots**: MakeHuman's hair cards read as a rough cap; the collar edge shows
  steps in extreme close-ups (the cut-in); folds are procedural, not simulated; the skin has
  no normal map of its own (the game's tiling pore map supplies detail).
- Reference: `docs/look/phase2/jin_before_after.jpg` and the stage stills beside it.

### Phase 2: all seven fighters (built 2026-10-30)

Kenji, Brutus, Mira, Lian, Rhea and Valka followed Jin. Every fighter is now a MakeHuman
model; the Quaternius bodies, hair pieces and generated outfits are no longer used (they stay
on disk, and in git history).

- **Outfits** come from `tools/mpfb/outfits.py`, a port of `tools/build_outfits.gd`'s
  garment language, so each fighter keeps the clothes it had: signed-distance regions on the
  T-pose body cut along their exact zero line (clean hems and necklines), trims split into
  their own colour slot, thickness per garment, folds on loose cotton that fade toward every
  edge (so layered garments don't cross), hems turned inward and shaded darker in the vertex
  colours, shoes and boots smoothed into a shoe shape with flat soles, a belt knot, Kenji's
  headband and Brutus's beard (cut from his face along an exact line, carrying the face
  shapes so it moves with the jaw).
- **Bodies**: male fighters on the male game skeleton, female fighters on the female one.
  Each JSON in `tools/mpfb/fighters/` picks macros, skin and mirror-match skin, eyes, brows,
  lashes and hair. Hair, brow and lash textures are made grey so the game's `hair_color`
  (unchanged from before) sets the colour.
- **Data**: `tools/mpfb/use_models.py <id> ...` points a character file at its model.
- Skin texture names come from each skin's `.mhmat` (some are `_diffuse3.png`).
- New `.import` files must be set lossy (`compress/mode=1`), which sim_test checks.
- sim_test checks the same promises on the new models: face shapes on every face piece,
  jaw and blink regions, gaze turning in place, hem shading, the second UV set, realistic
  shading, skinned fabric-shaded clothes in every slot with valid weights, mirror colours,
  matte cloth.
- Shoes and boots: the toes are separate surfaces with crevices between them, so each foot
  point is averaged with every point within 3 cm (four passes) before the soles go flat.
  Lian's jacket sits outside her trousers below the belt line (`over_below_waist`).
- **Cost**: two MakeHuman fighters run every stage at 67 to 85 fps at High (74 to 93 with the
  old bodies).
- **Known weak spots**: MakeHuman's hair cards (best on Rhea and Valka, roughest on the short
  male cuts), the beard is a shaped shell rather than strands, folds are procedural.
- Reference: `docs/look/phase2/fighters_compare.jpg`, `portraits_after.jpg`.

### Phase 3 pilot: the Boxing Ring (built 2026-10-30)

The pill crowd is gone. The Ring's stands hold about a thousand real people, and the floor
around the ring is dressed with modelled props.

- **Crowd**: twelve MakeHuman spectators (`tools/mpfb/crowd.json`: men and women of several
  ages and ethnicities in MakeHuman's casual, work, elegant and sport suits, one in a fedora)
  built by `tools/mpfb/build_crowd.py`. Each is rigged like a fighter, joined, its look
  baked into one 1024 px atlas in two colourways (the clothes hue-shifted, so the stands
  don't fill with one shirt), and decimated to 2,400 triangles, plus a 900-triangle level of
  detail for the back rows.
- **Animation**: four seated clips baked into vertex-animation textures at 15 frames a
  second: idle and talking from the Universal Animation Library, cheering and clapping posed
  on top of the idle. Each vertex's column is in its second UV set (two 6-bit halves, since
  Godot stores UV2 at half precision). Blender writes raw half floats (`*.vat`, not in git);
  `tools/crowd_vat_import.gd` turns them into Godot textures, because Godot's EXR reader
  garbles Blender's half-float EXRs.
- **Drawing**: `scripts/stages/crowd.gd` with `people` seats everyone on a moulded stadium
  seat (instanced), with aisles every twelve seats and empty seats here and there; a
  MultiMesh per model, colourway, side and detail level, drawn by
  `assets/crowd/crowd_vat.gdshader`. Each spectator has a phase, a talker or still-sitter
  calm clip, a cheer or clap loud clip, and a threshold: they get up when the stage's crowd
  excitement (StageAmbience) passes it, over half a second. Rows further back are a little
  darker.
- **Ringside**: Poly Haven CC0 models (`assets/stages/ring/props/`): corner stools, spit
  buckets, water, the cutman's kit and tape at both corners; a draped judges' table with a
  camcorder and three seated officials (crowd models that never cheer); bins, crates, boxes
  and fire extinguishers by the barricades. Concrete stands, fictional sponsor prints on the
  canvas, and a generated wear layer over it (scuffing where the fighters work, rubber marks,
  dried stains, grime along the ropes).
- `tools/render_stage_views.gd` renders free-camera stills (judges, red corner, stands,
  wide) at a given crowd excitement.
- **Cost**: the Ring runs at about 73 fps at High (75 with the old pill crowd). A Metal
  System Trace (`xcrun xctrace record --template "Metal System Trace"`) showed where the
  first version's 3 ms went: about 1.4 ms of fragment work in the main colour pass and 1.5 ms
  of vertex work. Engine lighting on the people was the expensive part; fewer triangles,
  smaller people, no shadows or no specular each moved it by less than run-to-run noise.
  So `crowd_vat.gdshader` is unshaded and lights the crowd itself: a sky/floor ambient,
  the stage's fill light, and an optional spill from the ring (`Crowd.people_*_light`),
  calibrated to within about 5% of the lit version's brightness on the stands
  (`docs/look/phase3/ring_crowd_lighting.jpg`). Only the front row uses the 2,400-triangle
  mesh; the rest use the 900-triangle level of detail (+2 fps, not visible at the fight
  camera). Runs vary by about 3 fps while other apps use the GPU; compare interleaved runs.
- Trade-off: the crowd gets no ambient occlusion, indirect light or shadows from the
  engine, so it reads slightly flatter in close-ups.
- **Not done in the pilot**: the barricades, apron and truss are still primitives; the far
  side of the arena beyond the stands is a dark void.
- Reference: `docs/look/phase3/ring_before_after.jpg`, `ring_fight_before_after.jpg`, and
  the close views `ring_judges.jpg`, `ring_red_corner.jpg`, `ring_stands.jpg`,
  `ring_wide.jpg`.

### Phase 3: the Temple (built 2026-10-30)

The second stage through the recipe. The Temple has no crowd, so the work went into its
trees, buildings and ground.

- **Cherry trees**: `tools/blender/build_cherry_tree.py` turns Poly Haven's Jacaranda Tree
  (CC0, about 3.9 million triangles) into a 51,000-triangle cherry: the trunk and branches
  decimated (40,000 for the branches; fewer left flat shards in the crown), and the 2.4
  million modelled leaves replaced by about 2,600 blossom cards on the canopy's outer
  shell. The cards use a drawn sakura atlas (`tools/blender/make_blossom_texture.py`: five-
  petal flowers with notched tips, eyes and stamens, buds, young leaves, twigs). The builder
  saves the meshes with the game's materials: bark tinted a cherry's dark red-brown, and the
  blossom in `foliage_wind.gdshader` (a gust that travels across the stage, a flutter per
  card, light from the low sun shining through).
- **Buildings**: generated hip roofs (four slopes that sag a little, corners that sweep up)
  on the pagoda's five tiers and the hall; rafter ends and fascia under every eave; bracket
  blocks; timber bands; shoji lattices over paper on every pagoda face and in the hall's
  bays, with lattice doors in the middle and a veranda. Weathered vermilion plaster on the
  torii and pillars, white plaster walls, dark planks and cedar.
- **Ground**: a dry rock garden (Poly Haven's Rock Moss Set 02 in six groups with ferns),
  shrubs along the walls, weeds where the gravel meets them; fallen petals scattered on the
  paving and drifted under each tree on the gravel; falling petals are now petal-shaped.
- **Mountains**: the same three rings, now with gullies and spurs and the Express's aerial
  rock-and-grass texture as detail, unlit in the old haze colours
  (`mountain_haze.gdshader`).
- **Cost**: 60 fps at High (76 before). The first version ran at 21 fps: the canopies had
  about 13,000 crossed cards each (about 24 layers of alpha-tested cards per canopy pixel),
  drawn again in every sun shadow pass. What fixed it, measured by taking parts out of the
  scene: only the canopy's outer shell gets cards, one card each, facing out (2,600 per
  tree); the blossom casts no shadow (the bark still casts the long sunset shadows); no
  alpha-to-coverage on the cards (+7 fps); the fallen petals cut out rather than blended
  (1.8 ms); small plants cast no shadows; the mountains unlit (0.6 ms).
- **Not done**: the lanterns and walls are still the builder's primitives (textured); the
  ground past the gate is a plain haze; the trees share one model, turned and scaled.
- Reference: `docs/look/phase3/temple_before_after.jpg`, `temple_fight_before_after.jpg`,
  and the close views `temple_tree.jpg`, `temple_gate.jpg`, `temple_hall.jpg`,
  `temple_pagoda.jpg`, `temple_wide.jpg`.

### Phase 3: the Dojo (built 2026-10-30)

- **Students**: the pill figures are gone. Eight MakeHuman students (`s1`–`s8` in
  `tools/mpfb/crowd.json`, men and women of several ages and backgrounds) wear outfits from
  the fighters' garment generator (`outfits.py`: `student_gi`, a training gi with a modest
  V and a belt, and `student_hakama`, the gi jacket over a pleated hakama). The atlas bake
  colours the generated slots (white gi; white, yellow, green, blue, brown or black belts;
  navy or black hakama) with the hem shading and a cotton weave; the second colourway
  swaps the belt or the hakama. A seiza pose is built in `build_crowd.py` (thighs sloping
  to the knees on the floor, shins flat behind, back straight, hands on the thighs) with
  four clips on top: breathing, whispering to a neighbour, a kiai fist, applause.
  `Crowd.people_kind = "student"` kneels them on the floor without seats; the Dojo now has
  a quiet crowd reaction so they respond to big moments.
- **Art**: the hanging scroll was blank (the label font has no kanji). `tools/make_dojo_art.py`
  draws 道 in brush ink with a red seal on aged paper in a brocade mount, and the taiko's
  heads in tanned hide with the mitsudomoe. The taiko is a turned, bulging barrel of
  lacquered staves with rings of studs and a pair of bachi on its stand.
- Exporting crowd meshes without vertex colours: the outfit's hem shading (already in the
  atlas) came through as black on the skin.
- **Cost**: 70 fps at High (68 before; the students are cheaper than the old figures).
- Reference: `docs/look/phase3/dojo_before_after.jpg`, `dojo_fight_before_after.jpg`, and
  `dojo_kamiza.jpg`, `dojo_taiko.jpg`, `dojo_students.jpg`, `dojo_wide.jpg`.

### Phase 3: the Express (built 2026-10-31)

- **Trees**: the cones and blobs are gone. `tools/make_train_art.py` draws a tree atlas

### Phase 4: effects and motion (built 2026-10-31)

- **Textures** (`tools/make_fx_art.py`, `assets/fx/`): a spark streak, a 16-frame impact
  burst (hot core, uneven rays, a shock ring), a 16-frame smoke puff shaded from above,
  and a footprint pressed into sand.
- **Hits** (`scripts/fx/fight_fx.gd`): sparks are streaks on crossed quads stretched along
  their flight and pulled into arcs; the impact burst is the flipbook, played over 0.2 s;
  both scale with the move's damage (0.6 for a jab to 2.2), counters 1.25 times bigger.
  Heavy hits and counters puff lit smoke.
- **Impact frames** (`scripts/fx/screen_fx.gd`, `assets/fx/screen_fx.gdshader`, a
  full-screen pass under the HUD that is hidden while idle): super finishers, K.O.s and
  heavy counters get two frames of a hard two-tone frame and a heat-distortion shockwave;
  other heavy hits and wall splats get the shockwave alone. Camera shake already scales
  with the hit's hitstop.
- **Supers**: the close-up is now a slow push-in through the freeze, everything but the
  fighter darkens (a soft ellipse kept bright around them), and the finishing hit plays
  in slow motion for a moment (not online).
- **Dust and debris**: knockdowns and wall splats raise smoke-textured dust in the stage's
  own colour (`Stage.dust_color`, set by each builder) and throw lit grit and pebbles. On
  the Beach (`Stage.footprints`) the fighters leave footprints that fade over 12 s.
- **K.O. replay** (`scripts/fx/ko_replay.gd`): the last 3.5 s of both skeletons are kept
  in a ring buffer; after a K.O. and its fall, the moment from 1.4 s before the K.O. plays
  at half speed, the camera orbiting low around it, letterboxed with a "REPLAY" tag, the
  K.O. burst and impact frame firing again as it lands. Then the round card. K.O. rounds
  hold `FightManager.KO_REPLAY_TICKS` (3.5 s) longer in every mode, part of the saved
  state, so match timing, rollback and saved replays stay deterministic; no replay
  shows online, in training, in replays or with the action camera off. The coach card
  waits for the replay, and its screenshot moves with it.
- The camera's Off mode also turns the screen effects off (the calmer setting).
- `tools/render_fx_check.gd` fires each effect in a live CPU fight and saves frames:
  `docs/look/phase4/`.
- **Cost**: nothing measurable when idle (Ring 70 fps before and after in an A/B run).
- **Not done**: no per-surface footprints on gravel (the Temple fights on stone).

### Phase 5: HUD and presentation (built 2026-10-31)

- **Typeface**: Barlow Condensed (SIL Open Font Licence, `assets/ui/fonts/` with its
  licence) is the project UI theme (`assets/ui/theme.tres`, built by
  `tools/build_ui_theme.gd`, `gui/theme/custom`): SemiBold for labels, Bold for buttons,

- **Health bars**: a lit gradient over a dark slab; fresh damage shows white for a moment
  (the chip) before the red trail drains; below a quarter health the bar turns
  red-orange.
- **Portraits** (the MakeHuman renders, `CharacterData.portrait`) in framed squares at the
  outer ends of the top row, P2's mirrored.
- **Timer, pips, meters**: a framed timer in the accent; square pips, gold when won;
  gradient super meters, gold when full, no longer pulsing (no moving lights on the HUD).
- **Combo counter**: hits and the damage the combo has done, real numbers.
- **Coach overlay**: corner talk on panels, chips as tags, a gold read badge, the round
  card restyled to match.
- **Round intro**: an opening crane shot of the stage with its name and line in the lower
  third, then each fighter as before (`FightIntro.STAGE_SECONDS`).
