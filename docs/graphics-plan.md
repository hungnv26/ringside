# Ringside graphics plan

Drafted 2026-10-30 at the user's request: "every element in the game is too basic; I want it to
look very detailed and comprehensible". The user chose **grounded realism** with fighters built
from **MakeHuman / MPFB2**. Phases 0 and 1 are built (2026-10-30); see "Progress" at the end.

## Where it stands

What the game is made of today, from the repo and from rendered stills of every stage and
fighter:

- **Fighters.** Every fighter is one of two Quaternius bodies (about 14,000 triangles, 2K
  textures), so all seven share two faces with different hair. Clothing is generated by
  `tools/build_outfits.gd` as a shell over the body: no folds, seams or weight, so a gi reads
  as plastic. Hands are low detail. Shading is already careful (wrapped diffuse for skin, eye
  catchlight, hair anisotropy, pore normal map) but is limited by the meshes underneath.
- **Stages.** Floors and walls use good scanned Poly Haven textures, but most props are
  primitives placed by the `tools/build_*_stage.gd` scripts: 154 primitive meshes on the Ring
  alone, cone trees and sphere clouds on the Express and Temple, box buildings, and spectators
  that are coloured pills. That is the main source of the "basic" look.
- **Lighting.** No ambient occlusion, no indirect light, no baked light, screen-space
  reflections off, one reflection probe on two stages. Objects float on the floor with no
  contact darkening, and every surface is lit the same way.
- **Effects.** Particle sparks, coloured rings and a limb ribbon. No textures, smoke, distortion
  or impact frames. Hair, belts and clothing are rigid.
- **HUD.** Godot's default font, flat bars, no portraits by the health bars.

The good news: the frame is already 1080p-plus, the stages use the right kind of textures, and
the shading code is built to take better meshes. The fix is better content, better light, and
motion on the parts that should move.

## Two decisions first

**1. Art direction.**

| Option | Look | Fits what exists | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|---|
| A. Grounded realism | Tekken 8: realistic people, scanned materials, cinematic light | Stages already use scanned textures; shading is already physically based | **Recommended** |
| B. Stylized detail | Street Fighter 6 / Arc System: painted textures, bold shapes, strong outlines | Keeps the Quaternius proportions; needs every stage repainted to match | Only if you prefer the style |

**2. Where the new fighters come from.** The repo is public, so every asset in it must be
redistributable (CC0 or CC BY), as today.

| Option | Quality | Licence in a public repo | Cost and risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. MakeHuman / MPFB2 in Blender | Realistic bodies, faces, skin, eyes, hair, clothes; one unique face per fighter | CC0 assets and exports: fine | Free. Needs Blender installed, a retarget of the current animations onto the new rig (Godot's humanoid retargeting), and the face blend shapes rebuilt. **Recommended** |
| 2. Upgrade the current Quaternius bodies | Better clothes, hair cards, textures, moving hair and belts; faces stay two | Already CC0 | Lowest risk, smallest lift |
| 3. Commercial characters (Character Creator 4, marketplace fighters) | Highest | Raw assets cannot be published: the repo would go private or keep them out of git | Paid licences |
| 4. AI-generated 3D (Meshy, Tripo, Rodin) | Fast unique looks | Depends on the plan's terms | Topology, rigs and faces are weak for close-up fighting; fine for background props |

## Phases

Each phase ends with before-and-after stills from fixed cameras (the existing
`tools/render_stage_thumbs.gd` and `tools/render_portraits.gd`) and a frame-rate check on this
Mac with `tools/bench_shading.gd`.

### Phase 0 · Baseline and budget (1 session)

- Capture the "before" set: every stage from the fight camera, every fighter close up.
- Measure frame time at 3200×1800 on the M1 Pro for each stage.
- Add a **Showcase** quality preset for coached matches and recordings. Recording uses Godot's
  movie writer at a fixed 60 fps, so a recorded match can use settings far too heavy for live
  play and still come out smooth.
- Price every effect and upscaler on this Mac before choosing presets.
- Install Blender if option 1 is chosen.

### Phase 1 · Light and rendering (1–2 sessions, lifts every stage at once)

- Ambient occlusion and screen-space indirect light, so fighters and props sit on the floor.
- Global illumination at Showcase (SDFGI). Baked lightmaps were dropped: Godot only bakes
  them from the editor, by hand, after giving every primitive a second UV set, which the stage
  builders would have to redo on every rebuild.
- Screen-space reflections where the floor is polished or wet: rooftop deck, dojo wood, ring.
- Soft shadows that sharpen at contact (light size on the key light), a larger shadow atlas.
- Upscaling that pays for the light: the 3D at 1080p, MetalFX spatial up to the window.
- A colour grade per stage (a lookup table), so each stage has its own mood.

### Phase 2 · Fighters (4–6 sessions, the biggest visible change)

- New bodies and heads from the chosen source: seven distinct faces with the expression blend
  shapes rebuilt (blink, squint, brow, jaw, smile, grimace), detailed hands, 4K skin textures.
- Modelled clothing with real folds, seams, stitching and belts, cloth materials from the
  existing Poly Haven fabric scans.
- Hair as layered cards with transparency instead of solid shells.
- **Secondary motion**: hair, belts, headbands, sashes and loose clothing swing with spring
  bones (Godot's SpringBoneSimulator3D). This is the single change that makes fighters feel
  alive.
- Real subsurface scattering on skin in the Showcase preset; the current stand-in stays for
  live play.
- Wear during a match: sweat sheen that builds over the rounds, scuff decals on hit areas.
- Re-render portraits and super cut-ins from the new models.

### Phase 3 · Stages (2 sessions for a pilot, then about 1 per stage)

- Pilot one stage end to end (the Ring or the Dojo), then apply the same recipe to the other
  seven.
- Replace primitive props with modelled CC0 assets: Poly Haven models and textures, ambientCG
  materials, Quaternius packs where a stylized piece fits. Cone trees, sphere clouds, box
  buildings and plank floors go.
- Decals for wear: scuffs, stains, cracks, posters, tape marks on the ring canvas.
- Foliage that moves in wind, real cloud layers or HDRI backplates instead of sphere clouds.
- **Crowds**: real low-poly people from the same character source, four or five cheering and
  idle loops baked into vertex animation and drawn with MultiMesh, with distant rows as
  impostor cards. No more pills.
- Background life per stage: passing traffic, flags, steam, birds, train scenery that streams.

### Phase 4 · Effects and motion (2 sessions)

- Hit sparks from textured flipbooks with shaded smoke, sized by damage and counter hits.
- Impact frames on heavy hits and supers: one or two frames of high contrast, a short
  heat-distortion shockwave, camera shake tuned per hit weight.
- Super moves: a slow camera push-in, background darkened, a brief slow-motion on the finisher.
- Dust and debris on knockdowns and wall splats, footprints on sand and gravel stages.
- A slow-motion replay of the K.O. from a cinematic angle before the round card.

### Phase 5 · HUD and presentation (1–2 sessions)

- A real typeface under the SIL Open Font licence for every label, set at proper sizes.
- Health bars with chip damage and a damage trail, fighter portraits beside the bars, styled
  round pips and combo counter.
- The corner talk, plan chips and round card restyled as panels that match the HUD.
- Round intro cameras that show each fighter and the stage before "Fight".
- Kept from the existing conventions: real data only, no neon, no strong glow, no moving
  lights on the HUD.

### Phase 6 · Polish loop (ongoing)

- Per-stage reviews against the before stills, performance checks, refreshed stage
  thumbnails, portraits and cut-ins, credits updated for every new asset.

## Constraints

- **Licences.** Every new asset is listed in `assets/CREDITS.md`; CC BY assets also on the
  in-game credits screen. Nothing that cannot be redistributed goes into the public repo.
- **Performance.** Live play must hold 60 fps on this Mac at the 1080p window; the Showcase
  preset and recordings may go further. Each phase checks frame time before it is committed.
- **Animations.** The Universal Animation Library moves stay; a new rig means retargeting, not
  re-animating. Gameplay and hit boxes are untouched by any phase.
- **Git LFS.** New textures and models go through LFS like the current ones; 4K textures add
  up, so stages share materials where they can.

## Recommended order

Phase 0, then Phase 1 (a big, cheap lift on every stage), then the Phase 3 pilot stage, then
Phase 2 (the longest), then Phases 4 and 5. Starting with light means the stage and fighter
work is judged under the final lighting instead of the current flat light.

## Progress

### Phases 0 and 1 (built 2026-10-30)

- `tools/look_capture.gd` shoots every stage from the fight camera (neutral and 6 s into a
  CPU-vs-CPU fight) and benchmarks each stage, in a coached match's window (1080p in screen
  points, 3200×1800 pixels here). `LOOK_*` variables switch single effects on to price them.
  Stills: `docs/look/before/`, `docs/look/after/` (High), `docs/look/showcase/`, and
  `docs/look/phase1_compare.jpg`.
- Prices on the M1 Pro at 3200×1800 native, from about 18 ms a frame: ambient occlusion
  +2.5 ms, screen-space indirect light +6 ms, reflections +5 ms, SDFGI +9 ms, VoxelGI +12 ms.
  FSR 2 cost more than it saved; MetalFX temporal broke with reflections (21 to 42 fps);
  MetalFX spatial at 60% doubled the frame rate and is nearly indistinguishable from native
  at the fight camera.
- **High** (default, live): the 3D at 1080p, MetalFX spatial up to the window, 4× MSAA,
  ambient occlusion with contact darkening, screen-space indirect light, reflections on the
  Dojo, Rooftop and Express floors, a slightly soft sun. 74 to 93 fps on every stage, up from
  53 to 73 fps before with none of it.
- **Showcase** (recordings): the window's full resolution, everything in High plus SDFGI and
  softer shadow filtering. 23 to 40 fps live; a recording renders every frame anyway. The
  setup page switches to it when *Record the match to video* is ticked.
- Per-stage light lives in `scripts/stages/stage_look.gd` (applied at run time to a copy of
  the stage's environment, so the generated stage scenes are untouched). The Ring keeps SDFGI
  off: its stands have no lamp and go black without the flat ambient light. A large sun
  (1.2°) washed the Temple's long shadows out, so suns stay at 0.3 to 0.35°.
- Coached matches take `--graphics=high|showcase`; the adapter passes `--graphics`, the setup
  page has *Picture quality*, tournaments carry it.
- Blender 5.2.3 is installed for Phase 2.
- What Phase 1 does not fix: at the fight camera the change is modest. The basic look is the
  content (primitive props, pill crowds, two shared faces), which is Phases 2 and 3.
